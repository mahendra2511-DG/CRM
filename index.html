Data Model

A layered Snowflake schema: Raw → Staging → Mart

Three fact tables at three different grains (lead, opportunity, product line) and two dimensions (account, user). Getting the grain right is what keeps revenue from tripling.

1 · End-to-end architecture

How the data travels, left to right: Salesforce exports are extracted and cleaned, loaded into Snowflake (Raw → Staging → Mart), turned into a model with KPI measures, and finally read by Power BI, Tableau and Excel. Your capstone uses the 5 exports (Account, Lead, Opportunity, Opportunity Product, User); the extra sources shown are the wider real-world picture.

2 · Snowflake staging schema

Every column and data type in the staging tables. The 🔑 key is each table's primary key (PK) and the 🔗 link is a foreign key (FK), so you can see exactly which column joins to which. Keep it open while you write SQL.

3 · How every table joins

fact_opportunity sits in the centre. Accounts and users join to it one-to-many, product lines hang off opportunity_id, and leads link in only after they convert (dashed line). The two mart views are these joins done for you. Get the grain right here or revenue gets counted more than once.

The specific traps interviewers ask about most in this model. Know these cold.

Watch out for this

Common QA mistakes on the trend KPIs

The four trend KPIs look like simple line charts, but each one has the same denominator and date traps as its scalar counterpart.

Before you sign off on any trend chart, check for these

  • Closed Won vs Total Closed Trend is Win Rate over time: if any month's denominator includes open opportunities, every point is wrong.
  • Expected vs Forecast Trend: Expected Amount is blank on 624 rows and Amount on 474; don't treat blanks as zero.
  • Active vs Total Opportunities Trend: trend by Created Date, or 'active' deals vanish the month they're created.
  • Closed Won vs Total Opportunities Trend: trend by Close Date, or you compare deals closed this month with deals merely opened.
  • Never add Opportunity Product to a revenue trend: it repeats Amount on every product line (3.1× for won deals with products).
  • Watch the date outliers: 13 deals close after 2022 and 7 in 2011; 244 won deals close before they were created.
  • Reconcile at least 3 points on each trend line against SQL: right in total but wrong by month still fails QA.

Interview Trap

Key difference: Win Rate vs. Opportunity Conversion Rate

Both use "Won Opportunities" in the numerator, so it's easy to assume they're interchangeable. They aren't: the denominator is the whole story, and it's the most common KPI mix-up in this project's QA round.

MetricFormulaWhat it measuresAXon value
Opportunity Conversion RateWon ÷ All created opportunities × 100How many of all opportunities ended as a win, including ones still open.1,443 ÷ 4,646 = 31.06%
Win RateWon ÷ (Won + Lost) × 100Sales effectiveness on finalised (closed) deals only.1,443 ÷ 3,374 = 42.77%
Lead Conversion RateConverted leads ÷ All leads × 100Marketing funnel: leads that became an account + opportunity. Often confused with win rate.1,033 ÷ 10,000 = 10.33%
